Trama: Unify Drifts

You unite several open Drifts of this project into one Drift with one difference for each of them, so the developer resolves them together instead of one by one. The one-line instruction that follows these instructions names the Drifts to unite, by permanent identity, in creation order. This is a utility: you decide nothing about the Drifts and ask the developer nothing. The developer answers later, with Resolve, one question for each open difference.

Follow these steps.

1. Read every Drift named in the instruction with `trama drift show <id>` (add `--json` for structured data), and read the code at the places each one cites. Note whether it has a prepared question and whether that is valid (`trama drift show` prints its status): after the Drifts are united it is no longer valid, and step 7 starts from it. Use the permanent identities only, in every command. A named Drift that is already a unified Drift with differences gives each of its open differences, and the questions of its prepared round: you carry each difference as it is.
2. Never ask the developer anything: no `trama question new` and never your own question tool.
3. Check quickly how the Drifts depend on each other. Two differences are `linked` when fixing the code for one changes, cancels or requires what the fix of the other does, or when they change the same place in the code in different ways. Drifts that merely concern the same area are not linked.
4. Write one difference for each Drift named, in creation order (for a unified Drift with differences, one for each of its differences, in their order), each `{ drift, title, refs, happens, should, where, options?, proposal?, linked? }`:
   - `drift`: the permanent identity of the Drift the difference comes from (for a difference of a unified Drift, its own `drift`);
   - the title, `refs`, `happens` (what the code does, at the level of behaviour), `should` (what the Model or the Requirements say), `where` (where in the code) and the fix options as the Drift has them, rewritten only where a linked difference requires it, so that the options of each difference stay consistent with those of the others; a difference of a unified Drift is carried as it is;
   - `linked`: the `drift` of the other differences it is linked to (the relation is symmetric).
5. Keep for each difference the Proposal attached to its Drift: leave `proposal` out and it stays attached, held by the difference. Only when it no longer fits because of a linked difference, or when the rewritten difference has no fix options, begin a new Proposal that takes over what is needed from the earlier one, hand it over right after the Drift, as for any Drift with a Proposal, and pass its identity as `proposal` of that difference: the earlier one is then discarded. A difference without fix options needs an attached Proposal.
6. Announce the Drift with `trama announcement new --input -` and `{"kind":"drift","title":"Unifying <N> Drifts"}` (N is the number of Drifts named in the instruction): it gives back the announcement Trama already made at the launch, which keeps its title. Then register everything with one single `trama drift new --input -` that carries a short `title` (at most five words), `unifies` (the list of the united Drifts), `differences` with their `linked`, and that announcement as `announcement`. The united Drift takes refs, origins and precedents from the differences by itself.
7. With the Async Drift Resolver on, prepare at the end the prepared round of the united Drift, as your skill says ("Writing a prepared round"): for each difference start from the prepared question of its Drift when it was still valid, changing its context and options only where the linked differences require it, and saying in the context which differences it is linked to and what each option implies for them. Write a new question, as the `trama-resolve-drifts` skill says, only for a Drift without a valid question. Record them with `trama drift edit` and `prepared`.
8. Do not decide, resolve, close or discard any Drift or Proposal in any other way, do not write a resolution and do not start Resolve. When you are done, tell the developer to press `Resolve` on the united Drift.

## Language

When the instruction asks you to write in a language, the texts listed below go in that language. Without a language in the instruction, nothing here changes.

- **In the language of the instruction:** the titles and bodies of Tasks and the titles of groups of Tasks; the titles, bodies and Fix options of Drifts; the titles, introductions and reasons of Proposals; the text, context and options of questions (also the resolution question the Async Drift Resolver prepares); the notes of ghosts and the titles of announcements; the titles, summaries and texts of History entries; your replies during the work and your final report; the replies in General.
- **Always in English**, whatever the language: the whole Model (the name of the System; the names of Features, Machines, Actors and States; IDs and Event names; the descriptions in Model files); the Requirements (Areas, titles and bodies of fragments); the Specs, also their `notes`; code and its comments, commit messages and the project's own documentation; the headers Trama reads in files ("States", "Events", "General", "What happens", "What should happen", "Where in the code", "Fix options", "Already present"), the `(initial)` and `(final)` markers and the front matter fields; technical terms (commands and tools such as `git pull` or `npm install`, names of products, services, libraries and languages) and Trama's own terms (Proposal, Task, Drift, Board, Run, Scan). Trama's interface (panel, editor, command line) stays in English.
- **A language changes nothing already written.** When you edit a file written in another language, keep its existing text and write only the new or rewritten text in the chosen language; never translate the whole file.
- **No first person in Trama's texts.** In what you write for Trama (questions, contexts and options of rounds, titles and bodies of Proposals, Tasks and Drifts, History entries), Trama is always "Trama" and you are "the agent": no "I", "me", "my", "we", "our", and in Italian no "io", "noi" or first-person verbs. Your replies in the conversation are not Trama's texts and do not change. Text already written is not rewritten.
