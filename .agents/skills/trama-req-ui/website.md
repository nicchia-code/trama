# Requirements: Interfaccia, Website

Use when building or changing the website: landing page, guide pages and their annotated screenshots.

## website

- Every page of the site is in English and shares one header: the Trama logo and name (link to the landing page), Guide, a link to the GitHub repository, an Install button that opens the extension on the VS Code Marketplace, and the switch between the light and the dark theme.
- The site has its own design, not a documentation theme. It uses the panel's palette (zinc base colour with a blue primary, light and dark, see `ui#General`). The theme starts from the visitor's system preference; the switch changes every page at once, and the choice is remembered in the browser for the next pages and visits.
- The landing page holds, in this order: a hero with the name, the tagline, the GIF of Trama at work (`docs/images/demo.gif`, the one of the README) and two calls to action, Read the guide and Install; the loop of Trama in five steps (describe a change, answer the agent's questions, approve the Proposal, run the Tasks, resolve the Drifts), one section per step, each with a short text and the view of the real panel that shows it; the requirements and the install; a footer with links to the guide, the repository, `docs/cli.md`, `docs/model-format.md` and the license.
- The five steps animate as the visitor scrolls: each section enters the screen with its text and its view of the panel sliding and fading in, and the view of the panel shows the step happening (for example a card arriving on the Board). An animation runs once, when its section first enters the screen.
- The guide pages have a sidebar with the four sections (Getting started, Concepts, Workflows, Panel & settings) and their pages, a table of contents of the page, and links to the previous and next page. A Reference group at the end of the sidebar links `docs/cli.md` and `docs/model-format.md` on GitHub, marked as external links.
- A step of a guide page shows its number in a circle, its short title and its sentences, with its annotated screenshot below, as wide as the text column.
- An annotated screenshot is the screenshot of the VS Code window with marks drawn over it in the style of Excalidraw: arrows with a slightly curved, uneven line, ellipses around a control, rectangles around an area and underlines, all with a rough hand-drawn stroke, and short notes (a few words, at most one line and a half) written in a hand-written font, Excalifont. Every arrow starts at its note and ends just beside what it points at, never over it; a note never covers what the screenshot is there to show.
- Marks and notes use one accent colour per theme, chosen to read on both the screenshot and the page: a warm red in the light theme, a warm yellow in the dark theme. Notes sit on the screenshot or in the margin beside it, with a soft background of the page's colour where the screenshot behind them is busy.
- The screenshot shown is the one of the site's current theme; switching the theme swaps every screenshot at once, keeping the marks in place.
- When an annotated screenshot first enters the screen, its marks draw themselves one after the other, as if traced by a pen, and its notes appear right after their arrows; the whole takes about one second and runs once.
- Clicking an annotated screenshot, or pressing Enter on it, opens it enlarged over a dimmed page, with its marks and notes scaled with it; Esc, a click outside it or its × close it and give the focus back to the screenshot. The pointer and a focus ring show that a screenshot can be enlarged.
- On a narrow window screenshots shrink with the column, and marks and notes shrink with them; below the width at which notes become hard to read, the notes move from the screenshot to a numbered list under it, and the marks show the numbers.
